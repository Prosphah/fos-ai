## The problem this was built to solve

You earn, and it doesn't accumulate.

You get paid. Bills come out. Something always remains, and something always gets eaten. By the time you look up, there isn't enough left to have built anything.

The gap between what you earn and what you keep usually isn't discipline. It's information. And financial education in Nigeria has an access problem, not a quality problem. The people who teach it well mostly teach it to people who already have money, and the people who teach everyone else usually have something to sell at the end of the lesson.

So here's what most people learn, one fragment at a time: someone says cut your spending. Someone else says invest more. Nobody shows you how those two decisions are connected, or which one has been quietly undoing the other.

You can be excellent at budgeting and have nothing to invest. You can earn more and end up in the same place. You can invest carefully and never notice you had no system for keeping anything in the first place.

## The eight lectures

### CODE 1 — MAKING MONEY
**Day 1**

**Lecture 1 · The different kinds of income**
Why earning more and building wealth are different problems. The seven types of income, from salary to royalties, and why most of the wealth on this continent comes from ownership rather than a payslip. We look at where the ten richest men in Africa actually got their money.

**Lecture 2 · Offline and online income streams**
What each one realistically looks like, what it costs to start, and how to pick one that fits your actual life rather than the one that sounds best.

### CODE 2 — KEEPING MONEY
**Days 2 and 3**

**Lecture 3 · Rule 101: pay yourself first**
Ten percent of everything you earn is yours to keep. Every other expense comes out of the remaining ninety. We work it through on a ₦100,000 month.

**Lecture 4 · Debt: identify, isolate, divide and conquer**
Good debt and bad debt. Why the highest-interest debt goes first even when it's the smallest, worked through with real numbers at ₦1,000,000. And the list of debts to run from.

**Lecture 5 · The two-day-and-twelve-hour rule, and your money system**
Where impulse spending actually comes from. The wait rule that cuts it. Budgeting you'll keep, why separate accounts make everything simpler, and the daily habit of recording what comes in and what goes out.

### CODE 3 — PUTTING MONEY TO WORK
**Days 4 to 8**

**Lecture 6 · Foundations, and the rules that keep you safe**
What investment actually is. Investing in yourself, in relationships, and in assets. The thirteen terms you need before anything else makes sense. How investments group by risk and by timeframe. Then the rules that matter more than the rest:

- Don't invest in anything you don't understand.
- Don't invest in an unregistered platform or app. Verify SEC registration first.
- Don't give anyone money to invest for you.
- Don't invest in anything promising outrageous returns for minimal risk.
- If it's too good to be true, it probably isn't.

**Lecture 7 · T-bills, bonds, mutual funds, REITs, and gold**

> This is the lecture most people come for.

**T-bills.** Lending to the government for 91, 182 or 364 days. What you actually pay and what you get back at maturity. Why there's a ₦50 million minimum on the primary market but you can start at ₦10,000 through apps.

**Bonds.** The same idea over a longer term, paying interest quarterly and returning your capital at maturity.

**Mutual funds.** A bucket. Money goes in, a professional invests it, the value updates daily. Equity versus money market, and which is which.

**REITs.** Real estate without buying a property. Why 90 percent of the income must legally go back to investors as dividends.

**Gold.** Purity and karat. The four tests for checking whether gold is genuine. Where to buy it. How to check the live price.

**Lecture 8 · Stocks, real estate, agriculture**
**Days 6 and 7**
**Stocks.** What owning a share means. Reading a company's size, profit and value using market cap, EPS and P/E. Dividend yield. What dollar-cost averaging does and when it doesn't help. US and Nigerian exchanges, and the sectors worth understanding.

**Real estate.** Buying and selling, land banking, rentals. The paperwork that decides whether a transaction is real: Certificate of Occupancy, deed of assignment, survey plan, governor's consent. This is where more people lose money than anywhere else on this list.

**Agriculture.** Cultivation, processing, storage, livestock, fisheries, forestry. And why it pairs with property rather than competing with it.

**And the finish.** How to sell yourself, your ideas and your business.

> **Bonus: hedging and selling — Day 8.** Forward contracts, futures, options and swaps — how you protect a position you can't control. Then how to sell yourself, your ideas and your business. Advanced, and included.

**From Lecture 4 — why the smallest debt often costs you the most**

> You owe ₦100,000 at 5 percent compounding, and ₦50,000 at 10 percent compounding. Both for five months.
> The ₦100,000 grows to **₦102,096.08**. The ₦50,000 grows to **₦52,122.08**.
> That gap looks trivial. Run it at ₦1,000,000 instead.
> At 10 percent you repay **₦1,042,440**. At 5 percent you repay **₦1,020,960**.
> The expensive debt costs ₦21,480 more, even though it's the smaller one.

**From Lecture 5 — the rule that does the most work**

> You've imagined what you'd buy with a fortune. Aliko Dangote has that fortune, and he isn't buying all of it. That's the whole point.
> On one rough estimate, if he held ₦500,000 in liquid cash, his daily spending would be about ₦250.
> So the most effective thing you can do about an impulse is **never buy instantly**. See something you feel you need, wait two days and twelve hours. If it's still there then, it's probably real. In my experience the feeling usually fades well before that.
