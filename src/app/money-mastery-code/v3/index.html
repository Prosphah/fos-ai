The problem this was built to solve

You earn, and it doesn’t accumulate.

You get paid. Bills come out. Something always remains, and something always gets eaten. By the time you look up, there isn’t enough left to have built anything.

The gap between what you earn and what you keep usually isn’t discipline. It’s information. And financial education in Nigeria has an access problem, not a quality problem. The people who teach it well mostly teach it to people who already have money, and the people who teach everyone else usually have something to sell at the end of the lesson.

So here’s what most people learn, one fragment at a time: someone says cut your spending. Someone else says invest more. Nobody shows you how those two decisions are connected, or which one has been quietly undoing the other.

  • You can be excellent at budgeting and have nothing to invest.
  • You can earn more and end up in the same place.
  • You can invest carefully and never notice you had no system for keeping anything in the first place.

The rule that does the most work

From Lecture 5

You’ve imagined what you’d buy with a fortune. Aliko Dangote has that fortune, and he isn’t buying all of it. That’s the whole point.

On one rough estimate, if he held ₦500,000 in liquid cash, his daily spending would be about ₦250.

So the most effective thing you can do about an impulse is never buy instantly. See something you feel you need, wait two days and twelve hours. If it’s still there then, it’s probably real. In my experience the feeling usually fades well before that.
