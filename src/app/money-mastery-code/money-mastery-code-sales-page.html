You don't have to be bad with money to be missing something important.

Earning money is only one part of building financial stability. What you do with the money you earn matters too.

The internet has no shortage of financial advice. It has a shortage of people who can tell good advice from bad advice.

Someone can sound confident and still be wrong. A popular investment can still be unsuitable for you. An impressive return can hide a level of risk you don't understand. You shouldn't have to blindly trust every person who talks about money online.

You need enough financial literacy to ask better questions, understand the trade-offs, and make decisions with your eyes open. That's what Money Mastery Code is designed to help you develop.

Meet your instructor

I care about what people can do with what they learn.

I'm Prosper Betiang, and Money Mastery Code grew out of a lesson I experienced firsthand.

A friend and I came into money around the same period. My friend received a larger share than I did. But my understanding of money helped me preserve and grow what I had, while my friend later found himself needing to borrow money from me.

That experience made something very clear to me: the amount of money you receive is not the only thing that shapes your financial outcome. What you understand about money matters too.

Since then, I've taught and spoken about financial literacy in different settings, including the Interswitch student community, Thrive financial conference, a church engagement, and events I've organized myself.

My approach is grounded in a simple belief: principles matter when you understand them well enough to apply them. I'm not interested in selling the fantasy of getting rich quickly. I want to break down complex ideas so that ordinary people can understand them, question what they hear, and make more deliberate financial decisions.
